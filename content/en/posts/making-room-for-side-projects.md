There is a particular kind of project that does not fit neatly into a roadmap. It starts as a question, a sketch, or a tool you wish existed. These projects are easy to postpone because they rarely have a deadline.

I have been trying a gentler approach: make the next step small enough to fit into an ordinary day.

## Give the project a home

Keep one note with the current question, the last thing you tried, and the next action. When you return after a week away, you can begin without reconstructing your context.

```text
Current question: What is the smallest useful version?
Last step: Collected three examples
Next step: Build a rough sketch
```

## Prefer a repeatable hour

An uninterrupted weekend is a rare resource. A regular hour is easier to protect. The point is not to make dramatic progress every time; it is to keep the thread from going cold.

Small projects grow through return visits. Leave yourself a clear next step, stop before you are completely drained, and let curiosity do some of the work.
